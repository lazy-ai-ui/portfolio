# Portfolio Writing Guidelines

## Purpose

Transform raw research notes, AI-generated drafts, or long explanations into concise, product-oriented portfolio copy.

The goal is not to shorten text mechanically, but to preserve the reasoning while making it easier to scan.

\---

# Target Audience

Primary:

* Product Design Leads
* Senior Product Designers
* Hiring Managers

Secondary:

* HR recruiters

Assume the reader spends 15–30 seconds on each section before deciding whether to continue.

\---

# Writing Principles

## 1\. Prioritize clarity over completeness

Keep only information that helps explain:

* the problem,
* the reasoning,
* the decision,
* the outcome.

Remove everything else.

\---

## 2\. Show product thinking

Every section should answer one of these questions:

* What problem existed?
* Why was it important?
* What insight was discovered?
* What decision was made?
* Why was this solution chosen?

If a sentence answers none of them, remove it.

\---

## 3\. Prefer evidence over adjectives

Avoid:

❌ intuitive
❌ innovative
❌ seamless
❌ powerful
❌ user-friendly
❌ efficient
❌ modern
❌ robust
❌ simple and intuitive

Instead describe facts.

Good:

"Interview participants struggled to locate recently viewed objects."

Bad:

"The navigation experience was confusing."

\---

## 4\. Remove AI writing patterns

Delete or rewrite phrases such as:

* After conducting research...
* Through comprehensive analysis...
* It became evident...
* This allowed users to...
* In order to...
* Leveraging...
* Utilizing...
* Significantly improved...
* Carefully designed...
* Robust solution...
* Powerful functionality...
* Seamless experience...
* User-centric...
* End-to-end...
* Comprehensive...

Rewrite using plain language.

\---

## 5\. One idea per sentence

Avoid long sentences.

Prefer:

Problem.

Insight.

Decision.

Result.

\---

## 6\. Be specific

Instead of

"Users needed a better navigation."

Write

"Engineers struggled to find infrastructure objects without knowing their exact names."

\---

## 7\. Keep the author's voice

Do not invent research.

Do not exaggerate impact.

Do not add fake metrics.

Only rewrite information already present.

\---

# JTBD

Never rewrite JTBD into generic UX language.

Keep the original intent.

Allowed:

* shorten wording
* improve readability

Not allowed:

* change the job
* change motivation
* simplify into "users wanted..."

\---

# Portfolio Style

The tone should be:

* calm
* confident
* analytical
* concise

Not:

* academic
* marketing
* corporate
* AI-generated

\---

# Length

Reduce text by approximately 30–50% whenever possible.

Keep only information necessary for understanding the design process.

\---

# Formatting

Prefer:

Short paragraphs.

Bullets.

Clear headings.

Avoid walls of text.

\---

# Before finishing

Check every paragraph.

Ask:

1. Is this useful?
2. Is this specific?
3. Is this easy to scan?
4. Does it sound human?
5. Does it explain product thinking?

If any answer is "no", rewrite it.

\---

# Final Goal

The reader should immediately understand:

* the problem,
* the reasoning,
* the product decisions,

without feeling that the text was generated by AI or written as an academic report.

# Golden Rule

Do not optimize for sounding smart.

Optimize for being understood.

If two versions communicate the same idea, always choose the simpler one.

The portfolio should feel like notes from an experienced product designer, not an article written by ChatGPT.
